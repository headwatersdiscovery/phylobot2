How to Read This Page

 {% include "phylobot/_uncertainty_explainer.html" %} {# A worked example (made-up numbers): how one column splits into the slices. #}
A worked example: two columns of one ancestor

Say a job ran two aligners, so each ancestor has two reconstructions, from MAFFT and from MUSCLE, weighted equally.

ColumnMAFFT's reconstructionMUSCLE's reconstructionTotalPosteriorAlignment
120K 0.6, R 0.4K 1.00.320.240.08 which residue
200L 1.0gap0.5000.50 residue or gap

Column 120. MAFFT's reconstruction is itself unsure: two draws from it differ with chance 1 − (0.6² + 0.4²) = 0.48. MUSCLE's is certain: 0. Their average, 0.24, is the posterior's part (blue): it would be there with one aligner alone. Pooled, the two reconstructions give K 0.8, R 0.2, so two draws from the pool differ with chance 1 − (0.8² + 0.2²) = 0.32; the rest, 0.08, is the alignment's part (solid red): the reconstructions lean to different residues.

Column 200. Each reconstruction is certain, so the posterior's part is 0. But MAFFT puts a residue there and MUSCLE a gap: pooled, half and half, so draws differ with chance 0.5, all of it the alignment's, hatched: whether the ancestor has a residue at this column at all.

An ancestor's pie adds up such numbers over all its columns (here 0.82: 0.24 blue, 0.08 solid red, 0.50 hatched red). With more choices (trees, models, gap placements) the part between reconstructions is split among them, each by its Shapley value. Compare → Most probable sequences keeps only the reconstructions' most probable states: the posterior's part is then 0, and every slice is the reconstructions disagreeing.

Steps 1 and 2 only say what the number means. Nothing is left to chance: it is the exact average over every pair of reconstructions (by their weights) and every sequence their posteriors could give, column by column, the value that repeating the picks forever would settle on. Bars are split the same way everywhere below, one colour per source. Hatched parts are disagreement over whether a residue is there at all; solid parts, over which residue. The choices' parts are Shapley values, so they add up exactly.

How It Is Computed

At each column a reconstruction is a distribution over the 20 residues and the gap. The chance that two independent draws differ, 1 − Σp², is the variance of the state written as a one-hot vector; summed over columns it is the expected number of positions at which two reconstructions differ. Pooled over the job's reconstructions (with the weights above) it splits exactly into the mean of each reconstruction's own variance (the posterior) and the variance between reconstructions. The two reconstructions are picked independently, so now and then the same one is picked twice: that is what makes the split exact (leaving those pairs out would inflate the choices' parts by n/(n − 1) for n reconstructions), and it is not where the posterior's part comes from, since every pair's samples carry their posteriors' uncertainty. The variance between reconstructions splits over the choices by their Shapley values: each choice's contribution averaged over every order in which the choices could be considered, so the parts add up whatever the design's balance. Residue or gap (hatched) and which residue (solid) are decomposed separately and add up to the whole.
