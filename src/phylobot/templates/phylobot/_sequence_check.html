Ambiguous Bases in Your Coding DNA

{{ a.codons }} codon{{ a.codons|pluralize }} in {{ a.sequences }} sequence{{ a.sequences|pluralize }} {{ a.codons|pluralize:"contains,contain" }} IUPAC ambiguity codes (R = A or G, Y = C or T, M = A or C, N = any base, …). PhyloBot keeps them as you gave them, and each step treats them as follows:

  • Translation (the aligners, amino-acid models and ancestors): the amino acid that every possible codon gives (CTR is Leu either way), otherwise X, unknown.{% if a.translated %} Here {{ a.translated }} of the {{ a.codons }} keep{{ a.translated|pluralize:"s," }} an amino acid.{% endif %}
  • PAML codeml weighs every codon the bases allow (GRG as GAG or GGG).
  • IQ-TREE's codon models treat the whole codon as unknown, as if it were NNN.
  • PRANK's codon aligner writes NNN in its place; PhyloBot puts your codon back.
  • Mutational paths: no change where the codon may be its ancestor's, otherwise an uncertain change (e.g. N64X).

A few ambiguous codons are likely to barely change the results. However, many in one sequence (a low-quality read, say) provide the codon models less information to learn.
